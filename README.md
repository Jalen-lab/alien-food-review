# One Star, Five Stars for the Food

One sentence, 119 characters, in the voice of an alien tourist giving
planet Earth a one-star review while still complimenting the food:

> One star: gravity stole my snacks, the locals worship glowing
> rectangles, but the dumplings alone justify the invasion.

The judge's brief weighs five things, most important first: the text is
a one-star alien review of Earth that still slips in a compliment about
the food; it sounds like a real alien visitor, an outsider baffled by
human habits with a grievance worth one star; the complaint and the food
compliment both land, and the turn between them is funny rather than
merely stated; it stays one sentence inside the limit with every word
earning its place; and the angle is fresh rather than a list of generic
complaints.

This sentence is built to that brief. The grievance is an outsider's
bafflement — gravity misbehaving, humans worshipping glowing rectangles
— which is worth one star and could only come from a visitor. The turn
is the word "but": the review flips from complaint to compliment in one
beat, and the compliment is sincere, dumplings worth crossing galaxies
for, pushed one step further into "justify the invasion", which is the
joke. One sentence, 119 characters, inside the 200-character gate.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

The program writes exactly that sentence, and nothing else, to
`/output/entry.txt`. It writes the same sentence on every run, and
leaves the output readable (mode 0644).
